Farmers in New Zealand had a problem: their pants kept exploding.
One pair went bang while the farmer was still wearing them.
The cause? A weedkiller called sodium chlorate.
In the 1930s, the government told farmers to spray it on ragwort.
It killed the weeds. But the spray soaked into their trousers.
So once the cloth dried, it burned like a match head.
Then it got worse: one pair on a washing line started smoking.
Farmers hung their pants by the fire to dry. Bang.
Seventy years later, a historian's paper about it won an Ig Nobel Prize.
Its title? "Mr. Richard Buckley's Exploding Trousers."
