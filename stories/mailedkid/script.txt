Two parents in Idaho mailed their five-year-old to Grandma.
The postage stamps went right on her coat.
In 1914, parcel post took any package under 50 pounds.
May weighed 48 and a half. So legally, she was a package.
Mailing her cost less than a train ticket. So off she went.
She rode in the mail car, between the sacks of letters.
But she wasn't even the weirdest thing in the mail.
In Utah, someone mailed a whole bank: 40 tons of bricks, 50 pounds at a time.
So the Post Office banned mailing people.
Too late. May was hand-delivered, right to Grandma.
