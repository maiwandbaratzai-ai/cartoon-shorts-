Commodore Fitz crossed the finish line backwards, sitting on his upside-down boat.
That morning, he'd sawed off its keel. "Less boat, more speed!" So why do sailboats have keels?
Fitz hung the keel on his wall, like a moose head.
Then he pointed at Nana June's boat. "June, your boat is wearing a shark costume!"
June didn't answer. Her pig, Truffle, was busy. He'd come for one thing: a suntan.
Leg one: the wind blew from behind. Fitz shot ahead like toast out of a toaster.
"Less boat, more speed!" he shouted, waving his hat.
Leg two: the wind blew from the side. June's boat kept sailing straight, like it was on rails.
But Fitz's boat slid sideways like a hockey puck, right through the fish market.

Then the wind pushed him all the way back to the start, which was also the finish.
So what was June's shark costume doing?
A keel is a big, flat fin under the boat.
When the wind shoves the boat sideways, the water shoves back on the fin, so the boat slides forward instead.

June won by a mile, and Truffle got his tan. Sunglasses and all.
