Big Lou's stolen pie car drove straight through his own barn.
He'd swapped its cone-shaped wheels for flat ones. "Easy as pie!" So why are train wheels cones?
The plan: with flat wheels, the pie car would get stuck at Hairpin Bend.
Fennel the fox only wanted one cherry pie. He'd picked it out a week ago.
[hype] Then the Pie Express hit the bend. The pie car screeched, threw sparks, and climbed the rail like a cat up a curtain.
It ignored the bend and rolled straight across Moo Meadow, past three confused cows.
Lou cheered. "Easy as pie!" Then he saw where it was going.
So what do cone wheels do?
Each wheel is fatter on the inside. On a bend, the axle slides out a little.
[beat] The outer wheel rides its fat part, the inner wheel its thin part, so the axle steers itself.
It's how a paper cup rolls in a circle.
Flat wheels can't steer. They only know straight.
Fennel got his pie. Lou got the other forty-nine. Easy as pie.
