Bea built her bridge full of gaps, with steel teeth sticking out.
You see, Red Canyon pays 5,000 dollars to the bridge that carries the mail all summer.
Her rival Duke laughed: "Is that a bridge, or a giant comb?"
Duke's bridge was smooth steel, end to end. Not one gap.
So every wagon picked Duke's bridge, while Bea's teeth went clack, clack, clack.
Then summer came in three steps. June: thirty degrees. Duke's bridge creaked.
July: forty degrees. A bump rose in the middle of his road. But Duke grinned: "Free speed bump!"
[hype] August: fifty degrees. The bump became a hump, and the mail wagon got stuck on top.
Duke pushed. The horses pulled. The mail did not move an inch.
So why did a steel bridge grow a hump?
Ever fought a stuck jar lid? Run it under hot water, and it pops right off.
Heat makes metal grow. A hundred meters of steel grows about five centimeters.
Duke's bridge had nowhere to grow, so it shoved the cliffs and bent up.
[beat] But Bea's teeth slide apart in winter, and together in summer.
So her bridge stayed flat as a table all summer long.
The mail rolled across, and Bea got the 5,000 dollars.
Duke begged: "Bea, can you put teeth in my bridge?"
[slow] She agreed. Her horse was already combing its tail on them.
