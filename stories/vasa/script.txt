In 1628, Sweden's mightiest warship sank within twenty minutes.
The Vasa carried sixty-four cannons and hundreds of carved statues.
But it was dangerously top-heavy.
In a test, thirty sailors ran across the deck. It nearly capsized.
It sailed anyway, as crowds cheered in Stockholm.
Then a gust tipped it, and water poured through the gun ports.
It sank in minutes, and about thirty people died.
Three hundred and thirty-three years later, it was raised almost intact.
Today, millions visit the warship that couldn't sail a mile.
