In nineteen ninety-nine, NASA lost a Mars probe to one tiny math mistake.
The Mars Climate Orbiter flew nine months to reach Mars.
Engineers steered it with small thruster burns.
But one team's software measured force in pounds.
NASA's navigators assumed newtons, the metric unit.
Nobody converted, so every push was four times bigger than they thought.
The orbiter drifted off course.
It arrived just fifty-seven kilometers above Mars, way too low.
It hit the atmosphere and was never heard from again.
The fix: one multiplication. The cost: a hundred and twenty-five million dollars.
