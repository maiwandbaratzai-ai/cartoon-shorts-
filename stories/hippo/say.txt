America almost turned Louisiana's swamps into hippo ranches.
The plan: hippos eat the weeds, then Americans eat the hippos.
The weed was water hyacinth, a purple flower from an eighteen eighty-four New Orleans fair.
It spread so fast that boats got stuck in the bayous.
Meanwhile, America was running short of meat.
So in nineteen ten, Congressman Robert Broo-sard wrote a bill: import hippos. Two problems, one animal.
Then it got stranger.
His star witnesses had been enemies in a war in Africa. One later became a German spy.
Now they teamed up to sell Congress on hippos.
A government expert said hippos could add a million tons of meat a year.
He said hippo tasted like pork and beef combined.
The New York Times called it "lake cow bacon."
Imagine ordering a hippo burger in New Orleans.
Even Theodore Roosevelt backed the idea.
So where are Louisiana's hippos?
Nowhere. The bill died in committee, and not one hippo ever arrived.
But hippos did make it to the Americas. Pablo Escobar kept four at his zoo in Colombia.
Today, well over a hundred of their descendants roam wild.
