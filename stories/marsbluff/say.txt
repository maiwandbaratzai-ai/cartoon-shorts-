In nineteen fifty-eight, a US bomber dropped an atomic bomb on a backyard.
It blew a playhouse to splinters and dug a crater seventy feet wide.
A warning light blinked, so the navigator went to check the bomb.
He reached for a locking pin, but the bomb came unhooked.
Two tons of explosive smashed straight through the bomb bay doors.
Then it fell fifteen thousand feet onto Mars Bluff, South Carolina.
Here's the part nobody saw coming: the nuclear core wasn't in it.
Six people were hurt, nobody died. The chickens weren't so lucky.
The Air Force paid fifty-four thousand dollars. For one backyard.
