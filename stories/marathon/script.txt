The 1904 Olympic marathon was won by a man on rat poison.
It was ninety degrees in St. Louis, on dusty dirt roads.
Cars drove ahead of the runners, kicking up clouds of dust.
One runner nearly died from breathing it in.
And there was just one water stop, at mile twelve.
Thirty-two men started. Only fourteen would finish.
Fred Lorz got cramps, so he hopped into a car,
rode eleven miles, then jumped out and jogged across the finish line in first place.
He was posing with the president's daughter when officials found out.
Meanwhile, a Cuban mailman had lost his travel money gambling and hitchhiked to the race.
He ran in street clothes, stopped to eat rotten apples, and took a nap.
He still finished fourth.
Another runner was chased a mile off course by wild dogs. He came ninth.
And the winner, Thomas Hicks? His trainers fed him strychnine, egg whites and brandy.
He was hallucinating, and they practically carried him to the finish.
His time was the slowest winning time in Olympic history.
