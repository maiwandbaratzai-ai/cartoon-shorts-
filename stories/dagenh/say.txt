At five A M, Sweden's drivers all switched sides of the road.
In nineteen sixty-seven, every car in the country stopped, crept right, and waited.
Sweden drove on the left. But most of its cars were built for the right.
In nineteen fifty-five, eighty-three percent of Swedes voted against switching.
So the government did it anyway.
Then it got worse: three hundred sixty thousand road signs had to flip in about a day.
Buses got new doors. A pop song begged, "Keep to the right, Svensson!"
Everyone expected crashes everywhere. Instead, crashes went down for a while.
Turns out, scared drivers are careful drivers.
