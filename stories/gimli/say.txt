In nineteen eighty-three, a brand-new jet ran out of fuel at forty-one thousand feet.
Both engines went silent, and most cockpit screens went black.
A tiny emergency propeller popped out of the belly, just to keep the controls working.
Sixty-nine people were now riding a giant paper airplane over Canada.
So how does a jet run out of fuel?
Canada was going metric, and the fuel got worked out in pounds, not kilograms.
So the plane took off with less than half the fuel it needed.
But the captain, Bob Pearson, happened to fly gliders for fun.
His co-pilot remembered an old air force base nearby: Gim-lee.
Then it got worse: Gim-lee wasn't an air base anymore.
Now it was a drag strip, with a car race and campers beside the runway.
The racers looked up and saw a silent airliner heading straight for them.
They came in way too high. So Pearson pulled a glider trick.
He flew the jet sideways to dump speed.
Then the nose gear collapsed, and sparks shot down the runway.
The jet skidded to a stop in front of the stunned racers.
Nobody was seriously hurt.
The plane flew for twenty-five more years, with a new name: the Gim-lee Glider.
