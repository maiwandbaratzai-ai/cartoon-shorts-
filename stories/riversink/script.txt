The Barnacle gang's boat crossed an ocean, then sank in a river.
It was carrying two tons of stolen gold bars, hidden under frozen fish.
At sea, Captain Gill loaded it right to the brim. "Every inch is money!"
Sir Whiskers, the ship's cat, wanted only one thing in life: dry paws.
The boat sat so low that waves licked the deck. Still, it floated.
The plan was to sneak up the Mudd River to their hideout, an old pickle factory.
But the moment it left the salty sea, the boat started sinking. Slowly, like it had changed its mind.

So Gill threw the fish overboard. "Every inch is money!" The fish thawed and swam home.
It kept sinking anyway, and the police waited on the riverbank with lemonade.
So how can a boat float at sea and sink in a river?
Sea water is full of salt, which makes it heavier. Heavier water pushes up harder.
[beat] River water is lighter, so the same boat sinks deeper. Load it to the brim at sea, and the river finishes the job.
That's why big ships wear a load line: a sea mark, and a higher river mark.
The gold went back to the bank. "Every inch is money," said Gill, from a very small cell.
